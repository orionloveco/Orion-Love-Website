Loma / Mack Seller Guide

Selling in Loma & Mack.

West of Fruita, Loma and Mack are mostly rural. Buyers here are buying land as much as a house, and they arrive with questions about it.

West of Fruita Acreage and outbuildings Water and access matter

Have an answer ready for the water question: irrigation, well or tap.

Market Read

Buyers want to know what the land can do.

In Loma and Mack, the questions go past the house: how much land is usable, what water comes with it, whether animals are allowed and how the road holds up in winter. Clear answers help a rural property sell with fewer delays.

Local Details

With rural property, I’d start with the water, the access and the land itself, then the house.

Water, access and outbuildings come before the kitchen.

Irrigation water, wells and shares can be confusing, and buyers and lenders both want them spelled out. Gathering water documents, well records and survey information before listing avoids surprises mid-sale.

A shop, barn or good fencing can add a lot of appeal for someone with horses or equipment. Showing how they’re used helps buyers picture themselves there.

Access counts as well. Road condition, easements and the drive to Fruita or Grand Junction all shape who the property suits.

Some buyers want a small place with room to breathe; others want working land. Deciding which kind of buyer your property suits keeps the price realistic and the listing aimed well.

Explain the land clearly and the house becomes the easy part.
