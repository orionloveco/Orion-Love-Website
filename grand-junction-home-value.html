How I price a home

Rule of thumb

Start with the house, then the street, then the market.

01–05

Five things I look at
before I give you a number.

I start with your house and work outward: the sales a buyer would compare it to, the listings they could buy instead, and the details that push the price up or down.

01

What buyers would compare

Recent sales a buyer would actually hold up against yours. That isn’t always the closest house, or the one with the same square footage.

02

What buyers can choose instead

The homes for sale right now. A sale from six months ago counts for less if three similar houses just listed down the street.

03

What condition changes

Which repairs or updates would move the price, and which ones you can skip.

04

What sets your home apart

A view, irrigation water, a bigger lot or a quiet street can each add to the price, as long as buyers in your price range care about it.

05

Where the price should sit

A range I’d be comfortable defending to a buyer’s agent and an appraiser, along with the sales that support it.

Local pricing context

Why countywide averages can’t price your home.

The Mesa County median is useful for the big picture, but no buyer shops the whole county. They compare a handful of homes in one area and one price range, so that’s what a value review compares too.
